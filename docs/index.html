Fire Districts & Drinking Water Service Areas

The area a drinking water system actually serves rarely lines up with the political boundary of the fire district or municipality that governs it. This map puts the two on top of each other so the gap can be measured.

What this map is not. These are service areas — where water is delivered — not the corporate or taxing boundaries of Vermont's fire and water districts. Those political boundaries do not exist as a statewide dataset anywhere; they were never centrally collected. The service areas are a defensible proxy only where a single district serves an entire town. Read the full data caveats →

    What this is

    The geographic area served by a drinking water system often does not align perfectly with the political boundaries of fire districts or municipalities. Today, the extent of properties inside a water district that are not served by a fire district is not fully known.

    This project closes that gap with a pilot mapping effort. We are working with selected fire districts to map their current political boundaries and compare them against drinking water service areas. Service areas are defined by the property parcels that intersect drinking water lines, as visualized in the ANR dashboard.

    Metrics

    1. Area difference — the spatial difference between a fire district's political boundary and the properties served by its water system.
    2. Coverage percentage — the share of the town's area, as defined by VCGI town boundaries, that falls within the fire district.

    Why it matters: the Lead & Copper Rule

    Under the EPA's updated Lead & Copper Rule Improvements, Vermont public water systems had to prepare a Service Line Inventory — documenting every connection from the water main to the building, including material type (lead, galvanized, copper, plastic, or unknown) — by October 16, 2024. DEC maintains guidance and templates requiring submission in standardized formats on its LCRR webpage. But Vermont lacks a comprehensive statewide service-area GIS layer linking water systems, their boundaries, and service line inventory data — which undermines planning for lead replacement, capital needs, and financing.

    What the Bond Bank would do with it

    Beyond regulatory compliance, the Vermont Bond Bank can leverage this mapped data for strategic capital planning and municipal bond financing. Overlaying service area boundaries with past and current infrastructure investments lets staff identify capital gaps, assess where previous funding has landed, and proactively plan future State Revolving Fund (SRF)-backed projects. The map also highlights regions with aging infrastructure and elevated risk, guiding outreach and technical assistance through programs like the Small System Capacity and Resiliency Program. Scenario modeling gets sharper too — municipalities with multiple flagged service areas could be bundled into a single financing package, improving project readiness and execution.

    It also contributes to risk management and equity. Mapping service line materials alongside system boundaries and demographic data helps ensure funding is targeted toward under-served and disproportionately affected communities, and it reduces the risk of delays and escalating costs by giving municipalities clearer visibility into the scope and urgency of service line replacement — particularly during early planning and bond-readiness sessions.

    Data sources

    • Water service areas — EPA Office of Research and Development, Public Water System Service Area Boundaries, filtered to Vermont PWSIDs. About 89% of Vermont's boundaries are authoritative (state or utility sourced) and 11% are EPA-modeled — far better than the ~60% national average. The layer panel lets you filter to authoritative geometry only.
    • Town boundaries — VCGI VT Data – Town Boundaries.
    • Service line inventory — Vermont DEC Lead and Copper Rule Revisions (contact: Rachel O'Reilly, Rachel.OReilly@vermont.gov).
    • Town clerk contacts — Vermont Secretary of State, Elections Division, Town Clerk Contact Information. Browse the clerk directory, or click any town on the map.
